# Voice-over prompt: NextUp demo (90 s)

Use this with a text-to-speech or AI voice tool (ElevenLabs, OpenAI TTS, etc.), or hand it to a voice actor. Export one 90-second file as `public/voiceover.mp3` and the video will play it automatically.

## Prompt

Record a voice-over for a 90-second product demo of **NextUp**, an AI agent that helps volunteer coordinators at small nonprofits cover shifts when volunteers cancel.

**Voice:** warm, clear and confident, like a friendly founder demoing their product. Natural and conversational, not salesy or hyped. Neutral accent, mid-range pitch.

**Pacing:** relaxed, about 140–150 words per minute. Each line must start at its timestamp and finish before the next one. Leave silence where there is no line. Short pause at each comma, slightly longer pause at each period.

**Emphasis:**
- Scenes 2–3 (the problem): a touch of empathy on "guilty group text" and "burned out".
- Scene 5: a confident beat on "One click." and a clear, deliberate read of "Nothing is sent until Alice approves."
- Scene 8: say "93%" as "ninety-three percent".
- Scene 9: slow down and land the final line as the sign-off.

**Pronunciation:** NextUp = "next up". Valley Community Pantry as written.

## Plain script (for TTS tools that take a single block of text)

Your volunteers, covered.

Hey, this is Michael. He volunteers for Valley Community Pantry's Saturday delivery run. Cancelling used to mean a guilty group text, and hoping someone sees it.

And this is Alice, the event coordinator. Every cancellation meant spreadsheets, a dozen texts, and asking the same reliable people until they burned out.

The agent proposes. The coordinator decides.

Michael can't make Saturday. One click. Within seconds, the agent finds people who are free, can drive, and haven't been overworked, and drafts a message for each. Every step is logged. Nothing is sent until Alice approves. The volunteer accepts from the email, and the shift is covered.

Alice can also just ask. The agent finds the shift and proposes only people who can drive.

It watches the forecast too. A storm on Saturday? The agent proposes alternatives, Alice picks one, and the whole crew is notified.

On the real model, ninety-three percent of scenario runs passed. And we watch every action the agent takes: only real volunteers, rules enforced in code, every step logged, and nothing sent without approval.

NextUp. The agent does the work. The coordinator stays in charge.
